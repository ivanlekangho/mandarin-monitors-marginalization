## Overview

This paper investigates how **digital platforms, state media, and language policy operate together as sociotechnical systems** to marginalize Uyghur linguistic expression in contemporary China. Uyghur linguistic repression has increasingly migrated from classrooms and local institutions into networked digital spaces — this paper traces how and why.

Rather than treating language policy and technology as separate domains, the analysis frames them as **interlocking mechanisms** that determine who may speak, in what language, and under what conditions.

---

## Research Questions

- How is social media weaponized as a tool of linguistic and cultural oppression against Uyghurs?
- How do digital platforms enforce Mandarin as the only legitimate public language?
- In what ways do Uyghur diaspora communities use digital spaces to resist linguistic erasure and reclaim cultural agency?

---

## Case Studies

| Case | Description |
|---|---|
| **State-scripted propaganda videos** | Analysis of 3,000+ coordinated videos in which Uyghurs repeat Mandarin-scripted talking points denying abuse, circulated via state and international platforms |
| **"Proof-of-life" coercive broadcasts** | CGTN and China Daily interviews filmed under surveillance, in Mandarin only, used to discipline dissent and intimidate diaspora activists |
| **Suppression of Uyghur digital spaces** | Dismantling of Uyghur-language blogs and the 2024 bans on Uyghur-language use on WeChat and Douyin, enforced through platform surveillance |
| **Diaspora resistance on social media** | Case study of @TouchofTurkestan (Instagram) illustrating how diaspora communities reclaim linguistic visibility outside China's surveillance regime |

## Theoretical Frameworks

| Framework | Scholar | Application |
|---|---|---|
| **Linguistic imperialism** | Phillipson | Explains institutional language hierarchies enforced through policy and infrastructure |
| **Linguistic capital** | Bourdieu | Analyzes how Mandarin becomes a coerced currency of legitimacy and access |
| **Linguistic economics** | Marschak; Wang | Shows how language choice affects economic participation and creates "language poverty" |

---

## Methods

- Literature-driven sociolinguistic analysis
- Close reading of archived propaganda and media content (via ProPublica, UHRP, and investigative journalism)
- Comparative analysis across state media, commercial platforms, and diaspora spaces

This project does not present new empirical datasets — its contribution lies in **theoretical synthesis and sociotechnical interpretation** of existing documented evidence.
